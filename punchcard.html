Wellgreens Dispensary · 90-Day Plan · Phase 2 · Item 16

First 10 Visits

A physical punch card that drives app downloads, repeat visits and bigger baskets — built so a forgotten card never costs you a customer.
Proposal · Prepared for Manny · September 2026

01The one decision that makes this work

Core principle
The card is not the ledger. Your POS is.

Every qualifying purchase is already logged against the customer's phone number. That count is the punch card. The physical card is the trophy they carry — it's marketing, not the record. Accept this one thing and the forgotten-card problem disappears, fraud stops mattering, and you don't need anything built inside the app.

What this buys you
  • Nobody loses progress. Card at home? Look up the phone number. Card in the washing machine? Reissue and punch it up to date.
  • Fraud stops paying. A stolen or photocopied card is worthless, because rewards are redeemed against the profile, not the cardboard.
  • You get the data. Visit frequency, basket size at each tier, which reward actually drives the return trip — none of which a punched card alone can tell you.
  • No app development. You said you couldn't figure out how to make it digital. You don't have to.

03Making the app download real

Three layers. Layer 1 is free and works on day one. Add the others as you go.

Layer 1 · Free
Issue only against an open app
The card is handed over when the budtender sees the app installed and logged in on the customer's phone. No app, no card. That's the whole gate, and it costs nothing.
  • Works on any POS, any app
  • Takes ten seconds
  • Needs one line in the SOP and one training session
Start here
Layer 2 · Recommended
Bind the card serial to the profile
Every card is serialized. At issue the budtender writes the customer's first name and last 4 of phone on the card, and drops the serial into their POS profile. A card with no matching profile is void.
  • Kills card sharing and "found" cards
  • Makes reissues clean
  • Gives you a real enrollment count
Add at launch
Layer 3 · Automatic
Platform app-install flag
If you run a loyalty platform (springbig, Alpine IQ, Dutchie Loyalty), it already knows whether that phone number has the app installed. Check the flag at redemption and the honor system disappears.
  • No budtender judgement needed
  • Also powers push notifications at visits 5 and 8
  • Requires the platform in section 04

04Option set A — where the count actually lives

Pick one. This is the ledger, so it has to be somewhere every store can see.

Option A1
POS loyalty points
One point per qualifying visit. Rewards redeemed manually at each tier. If your POS has a loyalty module, this is free and already in the budtender's workflow.
  • Cost: $0
  • Setup: an afternoon
  • Reporting: built in
Recommended if available
Option A2
Customer notes field
Budtender types into the profile notes: PUNCH: enrolled 9/20 · V4 10/1 · V5 10/8. Ugly, manual, and it works on literally any POS.
  • Cost: $0
  • Setup: same day
  • Reporting: none — you'd count by hand
Fallback
Option A3
Loyalty platform
springbig, Alpine IQ or similar. Automates tiers, texts customers at visit 5 and 8, and gives you the app-install flag from Layer 3.
  • Cost: monthly per location
  • Setup: 2–4 weeks
  • Reporting: full

05Option set B — the physical object

People forget cards. Almost nobody forgets their keys. That difference is worth more than the print cost.

Option B1
Wallet card
Standard credit-card size, heavy stock. Cheapest per unit, familiar to customers — and the one most likely to be left at home.
  • Cheapest
  • Most room for the rules
  • Highest forget rate
Budget option
Option B2
Key tag
Credit-card stock with a punched keyring hole, rounded corners, foil on the Wellgreens mark. It lives on their keys, so it's in the store every time they are.
  • Dramatically lower forget rate
  • Rules go on the back in small type
  • Doubles as a brand impression every time they drive
Recommended
Option B3
Combo — snap-apart
One perforated piece: a wallet card with a key tag that snaps out of it. Customer keeps whichever they'll actually carry, or the tag on keys and the card in the wallet as a spare.
  • Highest perceived value
  • Best chance one of the two survives
  • Slightly higher die cost
Best of both

06Option set C — how you mark each visit

The mark has to be hard to fake and easy to audit. Three ways, and you can run two together.

Option C1
Custom die-cut punch
A leaf-shaped hole punch — one per store, all identical. An office punch can't fake the shape, and the customer physically feels the reward being earned.
  • One-time cost per store
  • Tactile and satisfying
  • Can't be undone or erased
Classic
Option C2
Serialized holographic stickers
A roll of numbered foil stickers kept in the register drawer. One per visit. The roll is a controlled item — sticker count reconciles against POS punches at close.
  • Every punch is auditable against the drawer
  • Cannot be reproduced at home
  • Looks premium, feels like a reward
Recommended
Option C3
Rotating monthly stamp
Ink stamp whose design changes each month. A card punched with October's stamp in September is obviously wrong — regional managers can spot it across a counter.
  • Cheapest ongoing
  • Built-in date check
  • Twelve stamps per store to manage
Low-cost audit
Whatever you choose, add these two
  • Budtender initials and date in every box. Costs nothing, and it means every punch has a name attached to it.
  • Print the count on the receipt. "VISIT 4 OF 10 — 2 more to your free grinder." Now there are two records that have to agree, and the customer gets reminded even when the card is at home.

07Getting creative about collection

Small things that lift completion rates more than the rewards do.

Tactics worth stealing
  • "Leave it with us." An alphabetical card box behind the counter for regulars who know they'll forget. The card lives at their home store like a bar tab. These are exactly the customers who reach visit 10.
  • Make the milestones loud. Visits 6, 9 and 10 get a bell and a shout — the whole store hears "that's a free tumbler." Cheap theatre, and it sells the program to everyone else in line.
  • Head start. Punch visit 1 and 2 at enrollment. Starting at 20% complete instead of zero measurably lifts completion — people finish what's already begun.
  • Name the near-miss. Train one line: "You're on 4 — two more and the grinder's yours." Said at every single visit, it's the highest-leverage sentence in the program.
  • Stage the merch. Grinders and tumblers visible behind the counter, not in a drawer. People work toward things they can see.
  • Text at 5 and 8. One visit before a reward, not after. If you adopt a loyalty platform, this is automatic.
  • Give the budtenders a stake. Track enrollments by staff member and post it weekly. It ties straight into the Top Sales Rep KPI already on your dashboard.

08The forgotten card — your exact scenario

You asked: I buy on visit 4 without my card, and now it's visit 6. Here's the whole playbook.

Visit 4 — customer has no card
Budtender looks up the phone number. POS shows 4 visits, logs it, says "you're on 4 — bring the card next time and we'll catch it up." Nothing is lost, because the POS was always the record.
Visit 6 — card is back
POS shows 6. Budtender punches boxes 4, 5 and 6 in one go, hands over the grinder, marks the reward redeemed in the profile.
Visit 6 — still no card
POS shows 6. The grinder goes out anyway. Reward logged as redeemed in the profile. The card gets caught up whenever it next appears — and if it never does, nothing breaks.
Card lost or destroyed
Reissue on the spot. Old serial marked void in the profile, new serial written in, punched up to the current count. A caught-up card can never double-dip, because redeemed rewards are flagged in POS — not on cardboard.
"I've been here loads of times, I just never got a card"
Look up the phone number. Visits before the program started don't count — enrollment date is the start line, and the app has to be installed. Say it warmly, and punch visit 1 and 2 as a head start.
Customer shows up at a different store
Works everywhere. The profile is chain-wide, so all 11 stores see the same count. Say this out loud in training — it's a real selling point and staff won't assume it.
Two people sharing one card
The name and last 4 on the card have to match the profile at redemption. Mismatch means no reward. This is exactly why the card is bound to the profile at issue.
